This guide provides detailed examples for each production-note category. Use these to write notes that will generate high-quality Storymaker visuals.

## CAMERA: Composition & Movement

- ✓ "Static wide establishing shot of the entire warehouse, emphasizing emptiness and scale"
- ✓ "Static close-up of hands on the steering wheel, white-knuckled"
- ✓ "Static two-shot, seated across table, facing each other"

- ✓ "Slow push-in on her face as realization dawns"
- ✓ "Gradual pull-back revealing she's alone in a massive empty room"

### Pans & Tilts
- ✓ "Pan across the wall of photographs, each one a memory"
- ✓ "Tilt down from their face to their hands shaking"

### Tracking & Dolly
- ✓ "Tracking shot following her through the crowd"
- ✓ "Dolly in with her as she walks toward the door"

### Orbits & 360s
- ✓ "Slow 360-degree orbit around them, emphasizing isolation"
- ✓ "Orbit around the object, revealing detail from all angles"

### Handheld (Emotional/Chaotic)
- ✓ "Handheld shaky-cam capturing panic and disorientation"

### Lens Choice

- ✓ "Over-the-shoulder two-shot showing both faces in conversation"

### Depth-of-Field
- ✓ "Shallow DOF isolating foreground subject, background blurred"

## How to Write Strong Production Notes

### DO:
- ✓ Be specific: "harsh side-light from left" vs. "dramatic lighting"
- ✓ Use visual language: "trembling hands," "tears on cheek," "jaw clenched"
- ✓ Connect to emotion: "lighting suggests coldness in the relationship"
- ✓ Guide the AI: "slow push-in emphasizes her isolation" tells generation engine purpose
- ✓ Vary each scene: No two consecutive scenes have identical camera style
- ✓ Serve the story: Every note should illuminate character or plot

### DON'T:
- ✗ Be vague: "good lighting" or "emotional performance"
- ✗ Use internal states: "character is thinking about X" (show it physically instead)
- ✗ Assume knowledge: Explain lighting and camera choices explicitly
- ✗ Over-specify technical: "58mm lens at f/2.8" is too granular; let cinematographers decide
- ✗ Ignore continuity: Each scene should feel connected to last
- ✗ Make notes that contradict earlier dialogue/action

## Checklist: Production Notes Completeness

Every scene should include:
- [ ] CAMERA: At least one directional choice (static, push-in, pan, etc.) + framing approach
- [ ] LIGHTING: Time of day OR specific source + mood/color implication
- [ ] PERFORMANCE: At least one physicalized emotion or vocal choice per character
- [ ] AUDIO: Either ambient sound, music, or silence-as-choice (not just absence)
- [ ] VFX/EFFECTS: At least one visual element (may be "none required" for naturalistic scenes)
- [ ] CONTINUITY: At least one carry-forward from previous scene or promise of next

Notes should average 4-6 sentences total, specific but not excessive.
